\section{Asymptotic complexity analysis}
\label{app:comp.anly}

This appendix summarizes asymptotic costs. Decryption has the same order as encryption.
Let the padded working grid be $H_p\times W_p$ and define $N\triangleq H_pW_p$ processed symbols.
Let the block side be $b_s$ and $N_b\triangleq N/b_s^2$ blocks. Bounds are stated for fixed protocol
parameters and fixed $b_s$.

\subsection{Time complexity}

Each stage performs a constant number of full scans over $N$ symbols, or a scan over $N_b$ blocks
with $\Theta(1)$ work per block for fixed $b_s$:
(i) pixel-layer substitution--diffusion: $\Theta(N)$;
(ii) padding, lifting, and blockization: $\Theta(N)$;
(iii) affine block mapping: $\Theta(N_b)\cdot\Theta(b_s^2)=\Theta(N)$;
(iv) field-stream generation, masking, serialization, and EtM tagging: $\Theta(N)$ under the adopted packet format.
Hence,
\[
T_{\mathrm{enc}}(N)=\Theta(N),\qquad
T_{\mathrm{dec}}(N)=\Theta(N).
\]

\subsection{Space complexity}

The implementation stores a constant number of pixel- and field-domain arrays and buffers of total
size linear in $N$ (e.g., padded field state, mask, and serialized payload). Keys, headers, counters,
and wrapped parameters are $\Theta(1)$. Thus,
\[
S_{\mathrm{enc}}(N)=\Theta(N),\qquad
S_{\mathrm{dec}}(N)=\Theta(N).
\]

\section{Scoped confidentiality of the field-masked core (LoR/IND-style)}
\label{snote:indcpa_proof}

This appendix formalizes a scoped confidentiality statement for the isolated masked field-domain core.
It does not claim indistinguishability for the full packetized protocol, which additionally includes
canonical parsing/serialization, associated-data binding, and packet-boundary enforcement via EtM
under verify-then-decrypt.

Fix padded dimensions and let $n \triangleq H_pW_p$. Let $p=257$ and $\Fpf=\mathbb{F}_p$.
Let $T_K:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow \Fpf^{n}$ denote the deterministic keyed preprocessing map that
subsumes: (i) the pixel-layer transform, (ii) padding/lifting into $\Fpf$, and (iii) the invertible
block--field mapping. The masked core output is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:enc_core_indcpa_supp}
C \;=\; T_K(I)\;+\;R_{K_{\mathrm{str}}}(\nu)\pmod{p},
\end{equation}
where $\nu\in\{0,1\}^{64}$ is a nonce and $R_{K_{\mathrm{str}}}(\nu)\in\Fpf^{n}$ is a position-indexed
stream derived from a PRF/PRG keyed by $K_{\mathrm{str}}$.

\subsection{Nonce-respecting LoR/IND experiment for the isolated core}
\label{subsec:app_lor_exp}

An adversary $\mathcal{A}$ has oracle access to $\mathsf{Enc}(\cdot,\cdot)$ returning
\eqref{eq:enc_core_indcpa_supp}. Queries may be adaptive, under nonce uniqueness.

In the challenge, $\mathcal{A}$ submits $I_0,I_1\in\mathcal{M}$ and a fresh nonce $\nu^\star$ not used
before. The challenger samples $b\gets\{0,1\}$ and returns
\[
C^\star \gets T_K(I_b) + R_{K_{\mathrm{str}}}(\nu^\star)\pmod{p}.
\]
$\mathcal{A}$ outputs $b'$, and
\[
\Adv_{\mathcal{A}}
\triangleq
\left|\Pr[b'=b]-\tfrac{1}{2}\right|.
\]

\subsection{Assumptions}
\label{subsec:app_lor_assumptions}

(A1) \textbf{PRF-derived stream in $\Fpf^n$.}
The mapping $\nu \mapsto R_{K_{\mathrm{str}}}(\nu)\in\Fpf^n$ is a PRF over distinct nonces; i.e.,
$\{R_{K_{\mathrm{str}}}(\nu)\}_{\nu}$ is computationally indistinguishable from independent uniform
samples in $\Fpf^n$.

(A2) \textbf{Nonce-unique use.}
Nonces are not reused under a fixed $K_{\mathrm{str}}$.

\subsection{Theorem}
\label{subsec:app_lor_thm}

Under (A1)--(A2), the masked core \eqref{eq:enc_core_indcpa_supp} achieves nonce-respecting LoR/IND-style
confidentiality. For any efficient $\mathcal{A}$ making at most $q$ encryption queries (including the
challenge), there exists an efficient distinguisher $\mathcal{D}$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:indcpa_bound}
\Adv_{\mathcal{A}}
\;\le\;
\Adv^{\prf}_{\mathcal{D}}
\;+\;
\Pr[\Col_q],
\end{equation}
where $\Col_q$ is the event of a nonce collision among the $q$ queries. Under enforced nonce uniqueness,
$\Pr[\Col_q]=0$. If nonces are sampled uniformly from $\{0,1\}^{64}$ without stateful checking, then
$\Pr[\Col_q]\le \frac{q(q-1)}{2^{65}}$.

\subsection{Proof}
\label{subsec:app_lor_proof}

\textbf{Hybrid H0 (real).} Responses use $R_{K_{\mathrm{str}}}(\nu)$ as in \eqref{eq:enc_core_indcpa_supp}.

\textbf{Hybrid H1 (ideal mask).} For each fresh nonce $\nu$, replace $R_{K_{\mathrm{str}}}(\nu)$ by an
independent uniform $U(\nu)\gets \Fpf^n$ and answer $C=T_K(I)+U(\nu)\pmod{p}$. On repeated nonces, reuse
the same $U(\nu)$.

\textbf{H0 $\approx$ H1.} By (A1), any distinguisher between H0 and H1 yields a PRF distinguisher
$\mathcal{D}$ with advantage $\Adv^{\prf}_{\mathcal{D}}$, plus $\Pr[\Col_q]$ to account for collisions if
nonce uniqueness is not enforced.

\textbf{H1 gives zero LoR advantage.} For any fixed $I$, let $M=T_K(I)\in\Fpf^n$. In H1, $C=M+U\pmod{p}$
with $U$ uniform in $\Fpf^n$. Addition by a uniform group element is a bijection on $\Fpf^n$, hence $C$ is
uniform and independent of $M$. Therefore the challenge distribution is identical for $I_0$ and $I_1$,
implying $\Pr[b'=b]=\tfrac12$ and $\Adv_{\mathcal{A}}=0$ in H1. Combining the two steps yields
\eqref{eq:indcpa_bound}.

\section{Supplementary packet format and flow}
\label{app:flow}

This appendix gives a code-aligned summary of the packet layout and processing order.
A packet is $\mathcal{P}=(\mathrm{hdr},\mathsf{wp},\mathrm{blocks},\tau)$ with
$\mathrm{hdr}=(\texttt{ver},\texttt{kem\_epk},\texttt{salt},\nu,b_s,H,W,H_p,W_p)$.
Associated data $\mathrm{AD}$ is formed in canonical order as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ad_def} (including the
fixed version string \texttt{ver}), and the tag is computed by Eq.~\eqref{eq:etm_tag}.
The receiver enforces strict verify-then-decrypt: no unwrap and no inverse mapping are executed and no
plaintext is released unless $\tau$ verifies.

\subsection{Packet flow diagrams}

Figures~\ref{fig:flow_sender_compact} and~\ref{fig:flow_receiver_compact} summarize sender- and
receiver-side processing, emphasizing that authentication precedes parameter unwrapping and decryption.

\subsection{Throughput comparison and scheme summary}

Packet-compatible AEAD baselines (AES-GCM and ChaCha20-Poly1305) are evaluated under the same transport
envelope: identical header fields, canonical $\mathrm{AD}$, serialization rules, and verify-then-decrypt
acceptance policy; only the payload-protection mechanism differs. For PF257, the payload is serialized
uint16 prime-field ciphertext blocks authenticated by EtM with a 32-byte HMAC-SHA256 tag. For AEAD, the
payload is byte-oriented ciphertext with a 16-byte AEAD tag stored in the packet tag field. To keep the
envelope identical, AEAD derives an encryption key from the same per-packet shared secret and
\texttt{salt}:
\[
K_{\mathrm{aead}} \leftarrow \mathrm{HKDF}(ss,\texttt{salt},\texttt{"AEADKEY|"}\Vert\mathrm{ctx},32).
\]
This key is used only for baseline experiments and is not part of the proposed protocol.

Throughput is reported under (i) u8-equivalent normalization $B_{\mathrm{u8eq}}/t_{\mathrm{enc}}$ and
(ii) wire throughput $|\mathrm{SER}(\mathcal{P})|/t_{\mathrm{enc}}$, where $\mathrm{SER}(\mathcal{P})=\mathrm{AD}\Vert\texttt{blocks}\Vert\tau$ under the canonical field encodings (so it accounts for header/metadata and wrapped parameters via $\mathrm{AD}$, plus payload blocks and tag). We also report
$\rho_w \triangleq |\mathrm{SER}(\mathcal{P})|/B_{\mathrm{u8eq}}$.
Table~\ref{stab:exp11_scheme_summary} reports dataset means. Because PF257 transmits uint16 prime-field
blocks, it yields $\rho_w\approx 2$, whereas AEAD baselines remain byte-oriented with $\rho_w\approx 1$.
Under active-tampering trials, all schemes produced zero false accepts; with $N=64{,}512$ adversarial
packets per scheme, the 99\% Wilson UL on the false-accept probability is $1.03\times 10^{-4}$.
